01

Executive recommendation

Applyr's current Stage 0 has two scoring mechanisms that don't talk to each other: a deterministic gap classifier (does a JD line have a matching tag?) that decides Tier 1/Tier 2/Skip, and a separate five-criterion weighted score that decides a 0–100 number nobody downstream was reading until this week. Both were built by intuition and iteration against real misses, which is a defensible way to patch bugs and a bad way to build a measurement instrument.

The recommendation: replace the tag-matching gap classifier with a single behaviorally-anchored evidence scale, apply it per-requirement with importance weights derived from each JD's own emphasis signals (not a fixed global split), and let that one computation drive both the gate decisions and the published score. Hard, factual disqualifiers (a missing license, a named regulated-domain requirement with zero evidence, a role-category exclusion) stay outside the weighted sum entirely — they gate before scoring runs, the way must-have requirements function as gates in structured hiring practice. Everything else — including named tools and years-of-experience thresholds, both weaker signals than commonly assumed — contributes to the weighted score instead of vetoing it outright.

The floor itself (currently 70) is very likely miscalibrated. Real applicant-outcome data puts the plateau for interview callbacks around 50–60% requirement match, not 70–90%. Section 14 proposes revised bands and is explicit about which numbers are research-grounded and which are design inference awaiting real calibration data.

02

What "fit" means here

Definition. Fit is the degree to which Jason's documented work history — workExperience.md and the claims built from it — provides direct or genuinely transferable evidence of the knowledge, skills, abilities, and experience a specific employer has stated it needs for a specific role. It is a measurement of demonstrated capability against stated requirements. It is not a prediction, a popularity score, or a writing-quality score.

In scope

  • Demonstrated ability to perform the core work (behavioral evidence, not title or tenure)
  • Required skills, knowledge, and named tools
  • Scope, complexity, and ownership relative to what the role implies is needed
  • Named domain/industry knowledge, bounded (Section 9)
  • Recency, only as a confidence modifier on old evidence, not a separate scored dimension

Explicitly out of scope

  • Resume/cover-letter writing quality — that's conversion_rubric.md's job (R1–R8/C1–C5), a document-quality instrument, not a fit instrument. Keep them separate; don't let this rubric grade prose.
  • Likelihood of being hired, or of clearing an ATS keyword filter
  • Seniority/title labels, except as one input into scope evidence
  • Anything not written in the JD or documented in the candidate's ground truth

Eligibility, qualification, and competitive fit are three different questions and this rubric answers only the second and third. Eligibility (can Jason legally/logistically take the job) is handled upstream by the prefs/exclusion-zone gate and stays there. This document does not touch it.

05

JD requirement taxonomy

Applyr's current four buckets (required / preferred / responsibilities / culture) are a reasonable first split but too coarse to score against — "required" mixes a missing professional license with a nice-to-have tool. Decompose every extracted line into one of these types, derived from O*NET's KSAO framework and adapted to what actually recurs in real product-management JDs (empirically, from the 402-JD archive corpus reviewed this session):

TypeWhat it capturesScored how
Core DutyWhat the role actually does day to day (O*NET "tasks")Weighted, evidence-scored — the largest share of the Core Work Match sub-score
Credential/LegalDegree, license, clearance, work authorization — binary factsHard gate only, never weighted (Sec. 9)
Named DomainA specific regulated or specialized industry, paired with its own explicit tenure framing ("5+ years in payroll tax")Hard gate if unbridged and un-hedged (Sec. 9); otherwise weighted like a Core Duty
Named Tool/SkillA specific software product, technique, or methodologyWeighted, never a standalone gate (Sec. 9 — today's Bamboo Health fix)
General KnowledgeConceptual understanding without a named tool ("understands SQL," "familiar with Agile")Weighted, lower ceiling on evidence strength than a Core Duty
Scope/Ownership SignalTeam size, budget, user count, org level implied by the roleFeeds the Scope/Complexity sub-score, not scored as an independent requirement
Preferred/BonusAnything in a Preferred section or explicitly hedged ("a plus," "ideally")Weighted at a fixed low base weight (Sec. 10); never gates
Culture/ValuesCompany mission, "why work here," team-culture framingNot scored against candidate evidence at all — there is no valid way to have "evidence" of fitting a value statement

Importance signal, separated from wording

Per Phase 4's own instruction, wording alone (must/required/minimum vs. preferred/ideally/bonus) is a real signal but not sufficient. Combine it with: bucket placement (required section vs. preferred section), repetition (the same underlying competency named in both Responsibilities and Required — see Sec. 13 on deduplication before this counts), and prominence (first few bullets of a section, weakly, since JDs are not reliably ordered by importance — this is the lowest-confidence signal of the three and should never act alone).

06

Requirement importance model

Lawshe's Content Validity Ratio is the right instinct — item importance should be established by evidence of agreement, not asserted — but it requires a panel of independent expert raters, which a single automated evaluator doesn't have. The adapted version: derive importance from the JD's own internal signals rather than from panel agreement, and treat the derivation as a bounded proxy for criticality, not a claim of true CVR validity.

SignalWhat it indicatesWeight it contributes
Required-bucket placementEmployer marked this as necessary, not optionalBase weight tier (Sec. 10)
Cross-section repetition (after dedup)The employer keeps returning to this competency — centrality signal+1 weight step, capped once
Explicit hedge language present ("ideally," "a plus," "nice to have")Employer is signaling flexibility even inside a nominally required lineDowngrades one weight step and blocks hard-gate eligibility (Sec. 9)
Section prominence (early vs. late in a list)Very weak ordering signal, JD authorship is inconsistentTie-breaker only, never changes weight on its own
07

Candidate evidence scale

This replaces the current binary "does a tag from the anchor vocabulary appear near this phrase" check, which is keyword matching wearing a qualification-matching costume. Every level below is defined by a condition an evaluator can point to, not a felt strength.

LevelNameBehavioral definition
0No documented evidenceNothing in workExperience.md or the claim catalog addresses this requirement, directly or adjacently. Recorded as NO DOCUMENTED EVIDENCE, never silently treated as a low score — it's a distinct, explicit state.
1Adjacent evidenceCandidate has performed work sharing the underlying capability (per the 5-part transfer test, Sec. 8) but not the requested work itself.
2Partial direct evidenceCandidate has performed meaningful parts of the requirement but differs materially in scope, tooling, context, or level of ownership.
3Direct evidenceCandidate has clearly performed substantially equivalent work, at comparable scope.
4Strong direct evidenceSubstantially equivalent work with comparable-or-greater ownership, scope, complexity, or measurable outcome than the JD implies is needed.

A 5-point (0–4) scale was chosen over Jason's illustrative example unchanged: it's wide enough to separate "nothing," "adjacent," "partial," "direct," and "direct-plus-outcome" — the five states that actually recur across real claim ↔ requirement pairs in Applyr's own evidence maps — without adding levels fine enough that two raters would disagree about which one applies (the BARS literature's core failure mode). Collapsing to 4 levels loses the adjacent/partial distinction, which is exactly the distinction Applyr's transferability rules (Sec. 8) depend on.

08

Transferability rules

This formalizes the free-text bridge field already present in authoring_packet.json's evidence map into a required structure. Every claim of transfer (evidence level 1 or 2) must record all five parts below, or it isn't a valid transfer — it's an assertion.

1. JD requirement — the employer's line, verbatim 2. Candidate evidence — the specific claim_id and excerpt 3. Shared capability — the underlying skill both share, stated plainly 4. Material difference — what's genuinely not the same (scope, tooling, domain, level) 5. Why transfer is justified — the reasoning connecting 3 despite 4 6. Confidence reduction — how much interpretation this required (feeds Sec. 12)

Forbidden inferences

None of the following, alone, is evidence — per Jason's brief, restated as the hard rule it already is: a title ("was a PM"), an employer name ("worked at a SaaS company"), a company's size, an adjacent industry, a probable department interaction, a commonly-used tool the candidate likely touched, an implied capability from seniority, or trainability ("could probably learn it"). Potential is not evidence of demonstrated experience. Any of these appearing as the sole justification for a transfer invalidates the claim; it must be re-scored as level 0.

09

Hard-gate rules

A gate ends scoring for that requirement outright — it is not "worth zero points," it disqualifies the whole application regardless of how the rest scores. The research (EEOC job-relatedness requirement; must-haves function as gates in practice) supports gates existing. It does not support gating on everything an employer labels "required," and the years-of-experience findings (Sec. 3) argue specifically against gating on a bare number.

CategoryGates?Why
Credential/Legal (degree, license, clearance, authorization)Hard gateBinary fact, no substitute exists, direct EEOC job-relatedness precedent for licensure/credential minimums
Named Domain, unbridged and un-hedged, paired with explicit tenure framingHard gateFunctions as a credential in practice for regulated/specialized domains (payroll tax, healthcare revenue cycle) — the years framing is a bindingness signal, not a literal threshold check against the candidate's own tenure
Role-category exclusion (people management, AI/ML ownership, revenue/billing ownership, etc.)Hard gateNot a fit question at all — a factual mismatch in role type, orthogonal to how well-documented the candidate's evidence is
Named Tool/Skill, however phrased ("required," "must have")Never gates2026-08-19 finding: real posting (Bamboo Health) was rejected sight-unseen on one Tableau mention. Tools are learnable and substitutable; a conversation bridges this the way it can't bridge a missing degree.
Bare years-of-experience minimum (no named domain attached)Never gatesSackett ranking: years is one of the weakest validated predictors available; gating on it discards real opportunities the research says are worth pursuing
Anything in the Preferred bucketNever gatesBy definition optional; hedge language present is itself evidence against bindingness

Distinguishing a true hard requirement from a strong preference written as a requirement: a named domain/credential line only gates when it has (a) no product-management-experience alternative offered in the same line, and (b) no hedge language ("ideally," "preferred," "such as," "a plus"). Present either signal, and it demotes to a normal weighted Core Duty or Domain item — this is the exact rule that, tested live against the full 402-JD archive, correctly excluded 18 of 19 raw domain+years matches (axos_bank's "product management, consulting, banking, or fintech" is a strong preference dressed as a requirement; OneSource Virtual's "payroll tax filing" with zero alternative offered anywhere in the line is a true hard requirement).

10

Weighting model

Recommendation: hybrid. A small, fixed set of structural rules (research-grounded: required always outweighs preferred, hard-gated categories are removed from the weighted sum entirely) combined with per-JD dynamic weight derived from that JD's own emphasis signals (Sec. 6) — not a global fixed percentage split like the current five-criterion scheme. A purely fixed split can't distinguish a JD that spends six bullets on data-pipeline ownership from one that mentions it once in passing; a purely dynamic scheme has no way to guarantee required items always outrank preferred ones. Neither alone satisfies the brief's requirement that missing one essential item can't be bought back with volume on minor ones.

Requirement typeBase weightNotes
Required — Core Duty or Named Domain (non-gated)3
Required — Named Tool/Skill or General Knowledge3
Preferred — any type1
Repetition modifier (same competency, 2+ distinct sections, after dedup)+1Capped at one step; magnitude is design inference, needs calibration
Hedge-language modifier−1Applies inside an otherwise-required line carrying "ideally"/"a plus" language

The specific integers (3, 1, +1, −1) are design inference, not research-derived — the research supports the ordering (required > preferred, repeated > single-mention) but not these exact magnitudes. Flagged here explicitly per the brief's constraint against presenting invented numbers as established. They are a reasonable starting ratio and should be the first thing recalibrated once real outcome data exists.

11

The formula

Hard gates run first and are entirely outside this computation — if any fires, the result is DISQUALIFIED and the formula below never executes. Culture-bucket items are excluded from i entirely; they are never scored against evidence.

For each scored requirement i (not hard-gated, not culture): wi = importance weight (Sec. 10) ei = evidence level, 0-4 (Sec. 7) ci = confidence multiplier (Sec. 12: 1.00 high / 0.85 medium / 0.65 low) si = ei / 4 // normalize evidence to 0-1 RawFit = 100 × ( Σ(wi × si × ci) / Σ(wi) ) Sub-scores use the same formula restricted to each category's items: Core Work Match = RawFit over Core Duty items only Required Qualification = RawFit over required-bucket Skill/Knowledge/Domain items Preferred Match = RawFit over preferred-bucket items only

This is implementation-ready: every quantity is either read directly off a classified requirement (wi, from Sec. 10's table), assigned by the evidence rater (ei, from Sec. 7's five behavioral definitions), or assigned by the confidence rule (ci, from Sec. 12). No step requires an evaluator to interpret free-standing prose.

12

Confidence model

The brief is explicit that uncertainty must never silently become a negative score. A confident, unambiguous zero (JD is clear, evidence is genuinely absent) and an uncertain read (JD is vague, or the transfer required real interpretation) are different findings and must be visible as different findings.

LevelMultiplierApplies when
High1.00JD wording is unambiguous, and either the excerpt states the same activity nearly verbatim (evidence 3–4) or there is a clean, complete absence with nothing adjacent (evidence 0). Both a clear yes and a clear no are high confidence.
Medium0.85Evidence exists and a transfer judgment (Sec. 8) connects it, but the shared capability and material difference are both clearly articulable.
Low0.65Either the JD phrasing itself is genuinely ambiguous (a vague or compound clause admitting multiple readings), or the transfer bridge is a stretch that's hard to state cleanly.

Confidence is surfaced per-item in the output, not just folded into the number — a Tier 2 result built from five medium-confidence bridges should read differently to Jason than one built from five high-confidence direct matches, even at an identical RawFit.

13

Double-counting rules

Before weighting runs, cluster requirement items whose underlying competency is the same, even when the JD's own wording varies — "partner with engineering," "collaborate with technical stakeholders," and "work cross-functionally with engineering teams" are one competency wearing three sentences, not three independent scoring opportunities.

  • Clustering rule: two items cluster when they name the same skill/relationship/duty using different phrasing and neither adds a genuinely distinct scope, tool, or context. This is a judgment call the extraction pass makes once, up front — not something each evidence rating re-derives.
  • Scoring a cluster: the cluster is scored once, using its strongest instance's wording for classification (required beats preferred if either phrasing appeared in a required section) and the repetition modifier from Sec. 10 applied exactly once, regardless of how many times the JD repeated it.
  • Evidence reuse is fine, evidence inflation is not: the same claim_id may legitimately back multiple distinct clustered competencies (a single accomplishment can be evidence of both delivery execution and cross-functional collaboration). What's disallowed is treating five different phrasings of one requirement as five separate wins for the same claim.
14

Score bands

Bands are defined by evidence conditions first, RawFit ranges second — a number alone doesn't tell Jason what's actually true about the match.

BandRawFitEvidence condition
Tier 1 — Strong Fit≥ 75No hard gate; Core Work Match ≥ 70; direct or strong-direct evidence (level 3–4) across most required items
Tier 2 — Competitive Fit≥ 50No hard gate; core requirements covered at partial-or-better evidence, gaps concentrated in preferred items or bridgeable adjacent evidence
Stretch — visible, not queued35–49No hard gate; real gaps in required items, worth a look but not worth Stage 1 effort by default
Skip< 35 or hard-gatedHard gate fires regardless of RawFit; or RawFit below floor

These thresholds are the clearest example in this document of research-motivated design inference, not settled numbers. The TalentWorks data supports moving the floor down substantially from 70 — real applicants get real interviews around 40–60% requirement match — but that study measured raw requirement-count match, not this rubric's weighted evidence-and-confidence score, so the specific values 75/50/35 are a reasoned starting point, not a transplanted statistic. Calibrate these against real outcomes (which JDs scored where, and what actually happened) as soon as that data exists, the same way the current 70/72 number should have been checked and never was.

Worked examples

1

"5+ years of experience with Payroll Tax filing and/or Payroll Tax filing software."

Classification: Named Domain, required bucket, explicit tenure framing, no PM-alternative or hedge anywhere in the line.

Evidence: claim search returns nothing addressing payroll, tax, or filing software — NO DOCUMENTED EVIDENCE.

Result: Hard gate fires (Sec. 9). DISQUALIFIED regardless of every other item's score.

2

"Hands-on experience with Amplitude for product analytics"

Classification: Named Tool, required bucket.

Evidence: candidate has direct, documented experience with a different analytics platform (Pendo) at comparable scope, no Amplitude specifically.

Result: Does not gate (Sec. 9). Evidence level 1–2 (adjacent tool, same underlying capability), medium confidence. Contributes a below-average but nonzero term to RawFit — visible as a real gap, not a disqualifier.

3

"Own and lead product initiatives across the platform, from discovery through release"

Classification: Core Duty, required bucket.

Evidence: a claim documents owning product for a specific stack end to end, including discovery-through-delivery framing, at comparable or larger scale.

Result: Evidence level 4, high confidence. Full weight contribution.

17

Pressure test — candidates A–J

Generic, candidate-agnostic scenarios. Each tests one failure mode the rubric must resist or handle correctly.

A

Keyword match, weak substance

JD asks for "product strategy ownership." Candidate's document contains the words "strategy" and "roadmap" attached to thin, administrative-sounding activity.

Evidence rating is behaviorally anchored, not keyword-triggered — scores level 1, not level 3. Resisted.

B

Different vocabulary, strong substance

Same JD ask. Candidate's real accomplishment describes driving multi-quarter prioritization and roadmap decisions without ever using the word "strategy."

Semantic evidence-matching (Sec. 7–8) still credits level 3–4. Not penalized for vocabulary mismatch.

C

Adjacent domain, strong capability

JD wants healthcare-industry PM experience. Candidate has strong B2B SaaS PM experience with transferable regulated-data handling.

Domain item is soft (PM-alternative present in the JD line), not gated. Evidence level 1–2, medium confidence, partial credit.

D

Exact domain, weak capability

Candidate worked in the exact named industry, but documented evidence shows only administrative/support-level involvement, not the PM-level work asked for.

Evidence is scored on documented activity, not employer/industry label — stays at level 1 despite the domain match. Forbidden-inference rule (Sec. 8) holds.

E

Senior title, weak evidence

Candidate's title matches or exceeds the JD's seniority ask; documented accomplishments are thin and vague.

Evidence is scored on the documented accomplishment, never on title. Score stays modest. Resisted.

F

Lower title, strong evidence

Candidate's title reads junior; documented work shows ownership of a large-scale platform with cross-org influence.

Scope/ownership evidence (Sec. 7 level 4) is unaffected by title label. Credited fully.

G

Missing one genuine hard requirement

Excellent overall profile; zero evidence for a required professional license.

Hard gate (Sec. 9) fires. DISQUALIFIED regardless of RawFit — no amount of other strength buys this back.

H

Missing several preferred requirements

Strong core match; multiple preferred items at evidence level 0.

Preferred items carry base weight 1 vs. required's 3 (Sec. 10); RawFit stays high. Not disqualified.

I

High years, low relevance

12 years of experience, but in a substantially different function than the role's core work.

Years is not itself a scored dimension (Sec. 9); evidence is scored on documented activity, which is thin. Score reflects the real gap, unaffected by tenure.

J

Lower years, high depth

Only 3 years, but unusually large documented scope and ownership.

Scope/complexity evidence scores 3–4 on its own merits. Rewarded despite fewer nominal years — consistent with the Sackett findings on years' weak validity.

18

Failure modes

  • Single evaluator, no true inter-rater check. BARS' real reliability gains come partly from calibration sessions across multiple raters; Applyr has one (the model). Mitigation: the behavioral anchors themselves (Sec. 7) are the substitute for calibration — but this rubric's actual inter-rater reliability is unverified until it's run twice, independently, on the same JD and compared.
  • Thin JDs starve the model of real signal regardless of how good the rubric is — a JD with three vague bullets can't produce a trustworthy Tier 1 no matter how well the scoring runs. The decision table's thin-JD confidence cap (Sec. 15) mitigates but doesn't solve this.
  • The dynamic weighting model can be gamed by JD authorship style, not just role content — a verbose employer who repeats every requirement three times inflates weights that a terse-but-equally-serious employer wouldn't trigger. The dedup/clustering rule (Sec. 13) limits this but doesn't eliminate it.
  • Bands are not yet empirically calibrated (Sec. 14) — they're a reasoned starting point from real research, not a validated instrument. Treat early Tier 1 calls with some skepticism until enough real outcomes accumulate to check against.
  • The formula assumes classification quality — if the taxonomy step (Sec. 5) misclassifies a Core Duty as a Preferred item, the downstream math is correct but the input was wrong. Classification errors are a bigger risk to a correct final score than weighting-magnitude errors.

Rubric v1, consolidated

  2. Cluster duplicate-competency items (Sec. 13) before anything else runs.
  3. Run hard-gate check (Sec. 9): Credential/Legal, unbridged+unhedged Named Domain, role-category exclusion. Any hit → DISQUALIFIED, stop.
  4. For every remaining scoreable item: assign evidence level 0–4 (Sec. 7) using only documented, non-inferred evidence (Sec. 8), and a confidence multiplier (Sec. 12).
  5. Assign importance weight per Sec. 10's table, including the repetition/hedge modifiers.
  6. Compute RawFit and the three sub-scores via the formula in Sec. 11.
  7. Map to a band via Sec. 14, flagged as provisional pending empirical calibration.
  8. Report: band, RawFit, sub-scores, every hard gate checked (fired or not, with reason), and every scored item's evidence level + confidence + one-line justification — never just the final number.
