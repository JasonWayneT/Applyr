## What I Want vs. What I'll Take

**Genuine goal:** Consumer products. I want to build something people love to use and be part of that community. B2C is the long-term target.

**Immediate need:** Any IC PM role. With financial pressure, I can't be selective about sector. I will take a strong B2B role and use it to move closer to consumer over time.

**What I will NOT take:** Zero-to-one greenfield roles, Growth PM, Director/VP/management tracks. IC PM only.

## The Core Identity (works for both B2B and consumer)

> "PM who makes sure customers are heard in technical rooms, then makes sure what gets built actually solves their problem."

This is true at Cision (customer voice in a legacy B2B platform context) and true at Sterkly (consumer product, certificate bottleneck story). It transfers to consumer companies because customer empathy is the core B2C skill.

## Career Arc (three-act)

1. **Zero To Sixty Media (2017–2019):** Internal tooling and ops automation. Built from scratch. Account Manager / Product Owner.
2. **Sterkly (2019–2021):** B2C consumer software. macOS security tools, Safari extensions. Resolved a certificate bottleneck that was blocking ~$1–3M in product revenue.
3. **Cision (2021–2026):** B2B enterprise platform. $40M ARR, 3,500 accounts. Hired to rescue a platform others had failed to fix. Held 7% annual churn over four years under shrinking headcount and three executive transitions.

The arc shows range: internal tooling → consumer → enterprise B2B. Most PM candidates have a linear story. This one isn't linear but it's usable.

## What Makes the Story Distinctive

- The Cision context is unusual: hired *after* leadership abandoned a failed migration. Mandate was to keep customers on a platform being sunset. Not a growth story — a stewardship story. Almost nobody writes this.
- Sterkly is the B2C bridge. Real consumer product ownership, not just a side project.
- The "voice of the customer in a room full of engineers" framing is grounded in real decisions: the ticket prioritization system, the churn signal work, the data remediation initiative — all traced back to what customers were telling us.

## How to Frame for Different Audiences

**For B2B SaaS roles (primary target):**
Lead with Cision — $40M ARR, 3,500 accounts, 7% churn, technical platform depth. Sterkly is supporting detail.

**For consumer-adjacent roles (fintech, healthtech, productivity, consumer platform):**
Open with the identity ("customer-obsessed + technically literate"). Use Sterkly as the consumer credential, Cision as proof of scale and technical depth.

**For interviews: the B2C pivot story**
"I've always been drawn to building things people actually want to use. My B2C work was at Sterkly. What I'm looking for next is to do more of that — ideally at a company where I can be closer to the end user. My Cision experience gives me the technical platform depth and cross-functional range; I want to apply that in a consumer or consumer-adjacent context."

## Positioning Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- "Platform leader" / "product leadership role" — implies Director/VP, not IC PM
- "Global scale" — overclaims Cision's actual footprint
- "Deeply technical PM" — technically literate, not deeply technical. Don't invite scrutiny.
- "Standard PM role" — sounds like you'll take anything (defensive framing)
- Leading with B2C aspiration — not enough consumer proof points to win those roles outright
